Science Decision: What's Right For You
Goal: Fat loss primary · Muscle retention · Some growth · Calorie deficit
✅
Hypertrophy · PRIMARY training mode
75–80% 1RM · 8–12 reps · 2–3 min rest. In a calorie deficit, hypertrophy training is the only signal that tells your body to keep muscle instead of burning it (Barakat et al., 2020). Also produces the highest EPOC — your body burns fat for 24–48h after each session. This is your most productive modality for every goal you have.
✅
Strength · KEPT as foundation
85% 1RM · 3–5 reps · 4–5 min rest. You've built a 190kg squat and 180kg deadlift. Losing that neural base during a cut is a waste. Two strength days per week maintains the motor patterns and CNS output you've built. Mon Push + Fri Pull.
🔶
Isometrics · SUPPLEMENTARY only
Excellent for tendon health, joint stability, and end-range strength (the @lukamoves_ approach). Already embedded in every mobility session. Not a primary fat loss or hypertrophy driver — stay supplementary.
❌
Plyometrics · NOT now
Jumping and bounding at 143kg puts 3–7× bodyweight through knees and ankles per landing. Your knee history and current weight make this a genuine injury risk. Revisit at 110kg. The explosive intent on strength days gives you the RFD benefit without impact risk.
Periodization: WUP — Fat Loss Optimised
WUP (Rhea et al., 2002) — Daily variation prevents accommodation. Power day replaced with a second Hypertrophy day since plyometrics are out. This maximises the hypertrophy stimulus across 6 sessions while keeping two strength anchors to protect your base.

Progressive Overload — Exact Protocol
Double progression — reps first, then weight. Never skip steps.
Step 1 · Hit top of rep range on ALL sets
Target 4×8. Session 1 you get 8,8,7,6 — not ready. Session 3 you get 8,8,8,8 — ready to progress.
Step 2 · Add load, drop back to bottom of rep range
Squat/DL: +5kg → start back at 4×8 with new weight. Bench/OHP: +2.5kg. Isolations: +1–2.5kg.
Step 3 · Last set AMRAP every compound
Stop 1–2 reps before failure (RPE 8). This data tells you exactly when you're ready to progress. Log it every session without exception.
Step 4 · Deload every 4th week — mandatory
All compounds drop to 60% 1RM, 3×5, RPE 5 max. Connective tissue recovers slower than muscle. Skipping deloads causes tendon injuries, not fitness gains.

Calisthenics Integration Roadmap
Start alongside lifting. Skills build in parallel. No equipment needed beyond a pull-up bar.
Phase 1 · Now → Month 6 · Foundation
  • Negative pull-ups: jump to bar, lower in 5s ×5. Daily.
  • Dead hang: 3×30–60s. Builds grip and shoulder health.
  • Hollow body hold: 3×20–30s. Core prerequisite for everything.
  • L-sit on dip bars: 3×5–10s. Hip flexor + core integration.
  • Push-up variations: diamond, archer, slow-tempo 5s down ×3×10.
  • Scapular pull-ups: 3×10. Teaches shoulder blade control.
Phase 2 · Month 6–12 · Skill Building
  • Unassisted pull-ups: aim for 5 clean reps by month 6.
  • Dip progressions: bodyweight → add weight via belt.
  • Pike push-up → elevated pike push-up (handstand push-up progression).
  • Tuck front lever holds: 3×5s. Back and core integration.
  • Wall handstand: kick up to wall, hold 30–60s. Wrist + shoulder.
  • Ring rows: full horizontal, 3×10. Prerequisite for muscle-up.
Phase 3 · Month 12+ · Performance
  • Muscle-up: kipping first → strict progression.
  • Handstand push-up: wall-supported → freestanding.
  • Front lever: tuck → advanced tuck → straddle.
  • Human flag progression: side lever holds.
  • Planche: tuck planche lean → tuck planche hold.

Your squat and bench strength gives you exceptional foundation — your bodyweight drop from 143→85kg will unlock calisthenics skills faster than starting lean. By month 6 you'll be ~120kg which makes pull-ups, dips and L-sits very achievable.
